One Challenge Down, Another Awaits: BSCS Students Head to Software Testing

iConnect Publication
Septmber 7, 2026

As the Pre-Oral Defense closes, 26 BSCS groups leave the panel behind and move toward their next challenge—where their systems, not just their answers, will be put to the test.

MAMBUSAO, CAPIZ — From defending their ideas to standing behind the systems they built, fourth-year Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSCS) students continued their Pre-Oral Defense on September 3 before reaching the final day of the three-day defense series on September 7, 2026, at the Capiz State University–Mambusao Satellite College Accreditation Room.

Following the opening day on September 2, more student groups took their turn before members of the Computer Science faculty on the second day, presenting their projects, explaining the problems they sought to address, and responding to questions and recommendations from the panel.

The challenge continued on September 7 as the remaining groups stepped into the defense room for the final scheduled day.

Across the three defense dates, a total of 26 fourth-year BSCS groups were scheduled to present and defend their projects. Each group was challenged to go beyond demonstrating what their system could do and show how well they understood the work behind it.

Students had to explain the problems that led to their projects, justify the solutions they developed, discuss the decisions made throughout development, and respond to questions that tested both their technical knowledge and understanding of their work.

Members of the Computer Science faculty served as panelists throughout the Pre-Oral Defense, examining the projects and providing questions, observations, and recommendations aimed at identifying areas that could still be improved.

For the students, the three-day Pre-Oral Defense brought months of planning, development, coding, testing, troubleshooting, documentation, and preparation into the defense room. What had previously existed as ideas, diagrams, lines of code, and developing systems had to be presented as solutions they could explain and stand behind.

But reaching the final day does not mean the work ends here.

The questions, comments, and recommendations raised during the Pre-Oral Defense now become part of the next stage. Students are expected to address necessary revisions and further strengthen their systems based on the feedback received from the panel.

And beyond those revisions comes another test.

The students will next face Software Testing, where their systems will move from being defended through presentations and explanations to being evaluated through actual testing. The next stage will put the functionality and performance of their developed systems under closer examination and help determine whether they operate as intended.

The Pre-Oral Defense asked students to defend the decisions behind their systems. Software Testing will challenge the systems themselves.

From the opening presentations on September 2, through the continuation on September 3, to the final Pre-Oral Defense day on September 7, the experience has represented another significant step in the journey of the fourth-year BSCS students.

They have presented. They have answered. They have defended.

Now, their systems will be put to the test.
