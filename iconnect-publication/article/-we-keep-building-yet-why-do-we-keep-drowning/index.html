We Keep Building, Yet Why Do We Keep Drowning?

Mariel Jeane Rodico
August 23, 2026

With flood-control projects rising almost as fast as the floodwaters, one question refuses to sink: if we keep building solutions, why do Filipinos still need to learn how to swim through them?

Heavy rains brought by the Southwest Monsoon, or Habagat, continue to affect parts of the country leaving communities struggling with flooding and displacement. As floodwaters rise, families are forced to leave their homes and depend on temporary assistance while waiting for the water to recede.

The government responds through relief operations, providing affected communities with food, essential supplies, and other forms of assistance. Such aid is important during disasters, especially for families who have lost access to their homes and basic necessities. But when the same communities continue to experience flooding, a bigger question remains: 𝗜𝘀 𝘁𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗼𝗿𝗮𝗿𝘆 𝗿𝗲𝗹𝗶𝗲𝗳 𝗲𝗻𝗼𝘂𝗴𝗵?

Long-term solutions are also being pursued, including improving drainage systems, dredging waterways, expanding pumping capacity, and developing floodwater storage and retention projects. These measures are intended to reduce the risks faced by communities whenever heavy rains occur.

Yet repeated flooding raises a difficult question: 
"𝗪𝗲 𝗞𝗲𝗲𝗽 𝗕𝘂𝗶𝗹𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴, 𝗬𝗲𝘁 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗗𝗼 𝗪𝗲 𝗞𝗲𝗲𝗽 𝗗𝗿𝗼𝘄𝗻𝗶𝗻𝗴?"
Development means little if communities remain vulnerable to the same disaster year after year. Filipinos need immediate assistance when calamities strike, but they also need flood-control systems that work, infrastructure that is properly planned and maintained, and government projects that deliver the protection they are meant to provide.

Hindi lamang ang tubig ang kailangang pababain. Kailangang itaas din ang kalidad ng mga solusyong nagpoprotekta sa mamamayan.

We keep building. But until our communities are truly protected, we will keep drowning.
