Beyond the Code: BSCS Students Face the Panel as Oral Defense Begins

The iConnect Publication
September 2, 2026

The Oral Defense begins as 26 fourth-year BSCS groups face the Computer Science faculty to present, demonstrate, and defend the projects they have built.

MAMBUSAO, CAPIZ — The culmination of months of development, problem-solving, and preparation is put to the test as fourth-year Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSCS) students begin their Oral Defense today, September 2, 2026, at the Capiz State University–Mambusao Satellite College Accreditation Room.

A total of 26 student groups are set to face members of the Computer Science faculty throughout the three-day defense scheduled on September 2, 3, and 7, marking a crucial stage in their journey as graduating Computer Science students.

Beyond presenting their projects before the panel, the defense challenges the students to demonstrate how well they understand the work they have developed. Each group is expected to explain the concepts behind its project, justify key decisions, respond to questions, and demonstrate the technical and practical knowledge gained throughout the development process.

For the students, facing the panel also means going beyond what is written in their documentation or displayed on their screens. They must be prepared to stand behind their work—explaining not only what they built, but why they built it and how it addresses the problem it was designed to solve.

Members of the Computer Science faculty serve as panelists, examining the students' work, raising questions, identifying areas for improvement, and providing recommendations that may further strengthen their projects.

The Oral Defense represents one of the significant academic challenges for the fourth-year students. Months of planning, coding, testing, revising, and troubleshooting now lead to a different kind of challenge: communicating their ideas clearly and defending the decisions made throughout the development of their projects.

For some groups, the minutes before entering the defense room may be filled with final reviews and preparations. Inside, however, preparation gives way to presentation as students are called to demonstrate both the product of their work and the knowledge behind it.

With 26 groups scheduled across three defense dates, today's proceedings mark only the beginning. More fourth-year BSCS students will take their turn before the panel as the Oral Defense continues on September 3 and September 7.

As the first groups take their places before the Computer Science faculty today, the challenge extends beyond making a system work or presenting lines of code.

It is about showing that behind every project is a problem understood, a solution developed, and a group of future computing professionals prepared to defend the work they have built.


The defense has begun.
