Day Two: BSCS Oral Defense Continues as Students Take Their Turn Before the Panel

iConnect Publication
Septmber 3, 2026

The second day of the BSCS Oral Defense continued as more fourth-year students faced the panel to present their projects, defend their solutions, and demonstrate the knowledge behind their work.


MAMBUSAO, CAPIZ — The challenge continued for fourth-year Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (BSCS) students as another set of groups faced the Computer Science faculty on the second day of the Oral Defense, September 3, 2026, at the Capiz State University–Mambusao Satellite College Accreditation Room.

Following the opening day on September 2, the second day brought more student groups before the panel to present their projects, explain the ideas and processes behind their work, and respond to questions and recommendations from faculty members.

Inside the defense room, students were challenged to go beyond simply demonstrating that their projects work. They were expected to show their understanding of the problems they sought to address, justify the decisions they made during development, and explain how their proposed solutions respond to the needs identified in their studies.

Members of the Computer Science faculty continued to serve as panelists, examining each group's work through questions, observations, and recommendations. The process provides students with an opportunity not only to defend what they have developed but also to identify areas where their projects can still be strengthened.

For the students waiting for their turn, the second day carried the same mix of preparation, anticipation, and pressure. Final reviews continued outside the defense room, while inside, each group had the opportunity to demonstrate the knowledge and skills developed throughout their journey as Computer Science students.

With 26 groups participating across the three scheduled defense dates, September 3 marked another important stage in the ongoing Oral Defense.

The series is set to conclude on September 7, when the remaining groups take their turn before the panel.

From one group to the next, the challenge remains the same: understand the problem, stand behind the solution, and defend the work.

Day two may be over, but the defense is not.
